    Where to buy

    🏬 Big craft stores

    • Good for: yarn, hoops, scissors, glue
    • Tip: check weekly coupons and app-only discounts before you go
    • Buy yarn in the same dye lot for one piece

    📦 Online marketplaces

    • Good for: punch needles, gripper frames, monk's cloth by the yard
    • Tip: read reviews for “even weave” and “doesn't fray”
    • Bundles of needles + threaders are usually the cheapest start

    🧵 Independent fiber shops

    • Good for: premium wool for “boutique” pieces you'll price higher
    • Tip: ask for end-of-line or discontinued colors at a discount

    ♻️ Thrift & destash

    • Good for: yarn in bulk, frames, pillow covers
    • Tip: local buy-and-sell groups often have “craft destash” bundles for a few dollars

    🏷️ Wholesale (when you're selling)

    • Good for: monk's cloth bolts, cones of yarn, pillow inserts in packs
    • Tip: switch to wholesale once you sell 8–10 pieces a month — it cuts cost per piece

    🎁 Packaging

    • Kraft boxes, tissue paper, tags and twine in multi-packs
    • Nice packaging is cheap and lets you charge more (see Bonus 4)

    The $65 Pricing Formula

    The formula: (materials + finishing + packaging) + (hours × your hourly rate) + 15% for overhead and fees. Then round up to a “boutique” price ending in 5 or 9.

    How to justify $65+ without discounting

    • Finish the back cleanly and add a felt or fabric back.
    • Use wool or wool-blend yarn for the plushest “velvet” pieces.
    • Photograph it in daylight, styled on a sofa or shelf.
    • Offer a custom color or initial — custom always costs more.
    • Gift-ready packaging: box, tissue, a handwritten card.
    • Never apologise for the price. Say what it is and what's included.
